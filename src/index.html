🎮 Device Management

View and manage your connected devices. Assign custom prefixes (js1-jsN, gp1-gpN based on your settings) to override the auto-detected IDs. Prefix changes are saved automatically and persist between sessions, and will be used when assigning keybindings in the main Keybindings tab.

💡 Understanding Device Prefixes

Star Citizen identifies your devices as js1, js2, js3 (joysticks) or gp1, gp2 (gamepads). The app auto-detects these prefixes, but sometimes they can change:

  • After restarting: Windows may detect devices in a different order
  • With multiple devices: Device order can vary unpredictably
  • Mismatches: The auto-detected prefix might not match what Star Citizen shows

⚠️ Important: The prefix you set here is what gets recorded in your keybindings when you press a button on the device. If the prefix is wrong, your bindings will be assigned to the wrong device, and your controls won't work correctly in Star Citizen.

✅ Solution: Use the prefix dropdown on each device to manually set the prefix so that they match your in-game configuration. If you don't know, you can either bind something in Star Citizen to see which device it corresponds to, or experiment with the prefixes here until they match. Generally you can set your right stick to js1, and left to js2. You can always swap swap them on the keybindings page at any time.

🚀 Getting Started

Pages Explained

The app has 4 main pages:

  • Keybindings: This is where you'll be setting your actual bindings.
  • Visual Viewer: This loads a template, and displays your current bindings visually.
  • Template Editor: Create and edit device templates, assign buttons, and customize layouts.
  • Device Manager: Manage and configure your joystick and gamepad devices, including setting device prefixes to match your in-game configuration.
  • Character Manager: Manage your saved character profiles from the character creator - easily copy and backup your characters between various installations.

Common Workflow

  1. Create a template in the template editor for your devices (or skip this if you have one to import)
  2. Go to the keybindings page, import your keybindings file
  3. Go to the visual view, and load the template you created or imported
  4. Use the keybindings page to set your binds
  5. Use the visual view to check what buttons you have available, and what actions are bound to them
  6. While working save your keybidnings, and use the auto-save feature to quickly import your changes

❓ Frequently Asked Questions

What is a template?

Think of a template like a blueprint for your devices. It defines where buttons and controls are located, so the app knows how to visually represent them in the visual view. A template can have more than one devices within it, so you can create groups of devices.

The template editor is the page you'll use to create your layouts where you can assign the device prefixes to match your physical devices, add images of the devices, and arrange buttons to reflect your actual hardware.

Is my device supported?

I've done my best to try and make the app compatible with a wide range of devices, by reading the HID information directly from the deivces. Buttons and axes should generally work as expected. There may be some exceptions to this, but perfectly supporting EVERY possible device is a huge challenge.

Understanding Device Prefixes (JS1, JS2, GP1, etc)

Star Citizen uses "js1", "js2", "js3", etc. to identify different joystick/gamepad devices. This numbering system is crucial for proper binding management.

  • js1 = First joystick device as detected by star citizen
  • js2 = Second joystick device as detected by star citizen
  • gp1 = First gamepad device (Xbox controllers, etc.) as detected by star citizen

Your device prefixes must match your in-game configuration: If the prefixes are not correct when binding actions, you'll need to head to the Device Manager, and override them manually.

Joysticks Mirrored In-Game?

If your left and right joysticks are responding the opposite way they should (e.g., right stick movement is detected as left stick), follow these steps to fix it:

Step 1: Verify Device Prefixes

  1. Go to the Device Manager tab
  2. Check that your joystick devices have the correct prefixes assigned (typically js1, js2, etc.)
  3. If needed, use the prefix dropdown to manually override them to match your in-game configuration

Step 2: Swap Device Prefixes in Keybindings

  1. Go to the Keybindings tab
  2. Click the "🔄 Swap Device Prefixes" button in the left sidebar
  3. Select which two prefixes to swap (typically js1 and js2)
  4. This will swap all bindings between the two devices
  5. Save your keybindings file

Step 3: Swap in Star Citizen (if needed)

If swapping the prefixes doesn't resolve the issue, you can also swap them in-game without restarting:

  1. Open the Star Citizen console by pressing ~ (tilde)
  2. Enter the following command to swap joystick 1 and joystick 2:
pp_resortdevices joystick 1 2

Note: The Swap Device Prefixes method in step 2 is recommended as the primary solution, as it ensures your keybindings file matches your device configuration. The in-game console command is a fallback option if you prefer to swap them in Star Citizen instead.

📚 How-To Guides

How do I export my bindings from Star Citizen?

To export your current keybindings from Star Citizen:

Option A
  1. Open up the console with ~
  2. Type the command: pp_rebindkeys export all file_name (replacing file_name with your desired file name)
Option B:
  1. Launch Star Citizen and go to the main menu.
  2. Open the Options menu and navigate to the Keybindings tab.
  3. Click "advanced controls customiation"
  4. Click the "control profiles" dropdown
  5. Click the "save control settings" option, choose a name, and click save.
  6. Go to your Star Citizen user folder, and find the file you just exported.

How do I import my bindings into Star Citizen?

There are two ways to import your modified keybindings into Star Citizen. The Quick Way is recommended for the smoothest workflow.

⚡ Quick Way (Recommended)

This method uses the app's auto-save feature to deploy bindings to all installations and quickly reload them in-game:

  1. Configure your Star Citizen installation directory (if not already done):
    • Go to the Settings tab
    • Under "Star Citizen Installation Directory", click "Choose Folder" and select your Star Citizen base directory (the folder containing LIVE, PTU, etc.)
  2. Click the "⚙️ Auto Save Settings" button in the Keybindings tab to open the auto-save modal
  3. Enable the checkbox to automatically deploy keybindings to all detected Star Citizen installations
  4. Save your keybindings file using the "Save" button in the Keybindings tab
    • This automatically triggers deployment to all configured installations
  5. Click the "📋 Copy Command" button to copy the console command to your clipboard (located on the toolbar next to the "⚙️ Auto Save Settings" button)
  6. In Star Citizen, open the console by pressing ~ (tilde)
  7. Paste the command (Ctrl+V) and press Enter
  8. Your keybindings will be reloaded immediately in-game!

Example command: pp_RebindKeys layout_exported.xml

📋 Manual Way

If you prefer to manage files manually or don't want to use auto-save:

  1. Copy your keybindings file to a location outside the Star Citizen installation folders (for safekeeping)
  2. Manually copy the file back to your Star Citizen installation folder when ready (typically StarCitizen\LIVE\ or the relevant build folder)
  3. In Star Citizen, open the Options menu and navigate to the Keybindings tab
  4. Click the "Advanced Controls Customization" button
  5. Click the "Control Profiles" dropdown
  6. Find and select your keybinding file from the list
  7. Your keybindings will be loaded into the game

⚠️ Important Notes

  • First import not working? Sometimes Star Citizen requires importing the bindings twice. If your controls don't seem to apply on the first try, repeat the import process again—this often resolves the issue.
  • Auto-save vs. manual: Using auto-save is faster if you have multiple Star Citizen installations, as it deploys to all of them automatically when saving - super handy for fast iteration.

Applying Changes In-Game with Copy Command

Once you've made changes to your keybindings, you can apply them directly in Star Citizen without restarting the game:

  1. Save your keybindings file using the "Save Keybindings" button
  2. A "📋 Copy Command" button will appear in the top toolbar (it's only visible when you have a keybindings file loaded)
  3. Click the "📋 Copy Command" button to copy the console command to your clipboard
  4. In Star Citizen, open the console by pressing ~ (tilde)
  5. Paste the command (Ctrl+V) and press Enter
  6. Your keybindings will be reloaded immediately in-game!

Example command: pp_RebindKeys layout_exported.xml

⚡ Speed Up with Auto-Save

If you have multiple Star Citizen installations, the Auto Save to All Installations feature (accessible via "⚙️ Auto Save Settings") can dramatically speed up your workflow:

  • When enabled, your keybindings are automatically saved to all detected Star Citizen installations
  • This means you only need to enter the console command once, and all your installations will use the updated keybindings
  • Perfect for maintaining consistent controls across multiple game clients or testing on different patches

Tip: Configure your Star Citizen directory via the "⚙️ Auto Save Settings" button to enable this feature.

💡 Tips & Best Practices

Keybindings Page Tips

Before editing keybindings:

  1. The device prefix (js1, js2, etc.) is auto-detected, but can be overridden in the Device Manager if needed.
  2. When you press a button to bind an action, the app will assign it using the prefix you've configured for that device.
  3. Each action when hovered, shows various buttons to manage it - such as the manager, clear, reset, and bind buttons.
  4. Each action shows tags indicating what buttons are bound to it.
  5. You can click the button tag to search for it in the visual view. If found, it'll highlight the button frame for that button ID.
  6. If you click the button manager, you can set the button type per button. For example you can set a button to require double-tapping it, holding it, etc.
  7. Action rows that have a accented background are actions that YOU have customized - rows that have the default background are unmodified.
  8. If you're importing a key binding file for the first time, and all the prefixes are flipped - you can use the "swap device prefixes" button to correct them.

Generate Unbind Profile: This will generate a binding file for the very specific purpose of unbinding all key binds. THIS CAN HAVE UNINTENDED CONSEQUENCES, such as device IDs breaking. If this happens, a game restart will generally fix it. Use this feature with caution, and only when necessary.

Why are device prefixes important? If device prefixes are incorrect, your bindings will be assigned to the wrong device, causing controls to not work correctly in Star Citizen.

Visual View Tips

The Visual View is where you can see your keybindings displayed directly on your joystick template:

  • Requirements: You need both a keybindings file loaded (from the Keybindings tab) and at least one template selected to see anything.
  • Color Coding: Custom bindings appear in the accent color (usually cyan), while default Star Citizen bindings appear in grey. You can change this in the Display Configuration menu (⚙️ Config button).
  • Click Button Frames: Click any button frame on the joystick to open a modal showing:
    • The button ID (e.g., js1_button1)
    • All actions currently bound to that button
    • Options to remove individual bindings or add new ones
  • Quick Search: Click the button ID in the button modal to search for that button in the Keybindings tab.
  • Add Bindings Fast: Click the "Add Action" button in the button modal to open a quick search dialog for binding new actions to that button.
  • Multiple Templates: Load multiple templates to see all your devices. Each device will have its own page that you can navigate with Tab/Shift+Tab.
  • Customize Display: Use the Config menu (⚙️) to adjust button frame sizes, font sizes, and the number of lines shown per button.
  • Export as Image: Click the "Export" button to save a PNG image of the currently displayed device, perfect for sharing your layout with others.

Template Editor Tips

When creating a joystick template:

  1. Set the Device Prefix: When adding a device to your template, set the prefix to match how Star Citizen detects your device (typically js1, js2, etc., or gp1, gp2 for gamepads).
  2. This tells the template which physical device the buttons belong to when you're editing your keybindings.
  3. The prefix you set here should match your actual in-game device configuration.
  4. Navigate Pages Quickly: Press Tab to move forward through pages, or Shift+Tab to move backward. Pages loop around.

Tip: If you have a dual stick setup (e.g., VKB Gladiator), create one device per physical stick to keep your layout organized!

🔧 Troubleshooting

My buttons are mapping to the wrong device

This is where things can get a little tricky and confusing. As mentioned above, the main concern here are device prefixes. Here's the steps I'd recommend trying:

  • Record what prefix your devices are detected as in star citizen.
  • Head over to the Device Manager page
  • Use the drop down to override their prefix to match your in-game configuration
  • Return to the Keybindings page and try binding again
  • Remember, the prefix you set here is what gets recorded in your keybindings when you press a button on the device. If the prefix is wrong, your bindings will be assigned to the wrong device, and your controls won't work correctly in Star Citizen.

Common Issues & Solutions

  • Buttons not working in-game? Check your device prefixes in the Device Manager. The js numbers in your bindings file must match how Star Citizen sees your devices.
  • Bindings not working in-game? Make sure there isn't a similar binding with different wording already set. Star Citizen includes many legacy actions that may no longer function—check for duplicate bindings with slightly different names.
  • Button still active after unbinding? If you bind a button to an action and then unbind it, the button may still be bound in-game. You can either manually unbind that specific button in the game's control settings, or clear all bindings for that device: Advanced Control Customization → Control Profiles → Clear All Device Bindings.
  • Wrong device responding? Reconfigure joystick mapping and make sure to test each device.
  • Lost your mapping? The app stores device mapping in localStorage, but you can always reconfigure it.
  • Template buttons look wrong? Use the mirror template feature or adjust image flip settings.
